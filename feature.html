In an era of disposable thumbnails, we pause to document the architecture of a winning streak.

01 — The Architecture

A New Era

The 2025/2026 season marks a paradigm shift in the history of FC Porto. Following the seismic election of André Villas-Boas, the club embarked on a project of aggressive modernization. At the center stands Francesco Farioli. At 36, the Italian tactician represents the antithesis of the old regime—a philosopher of the game tasked with marrying historical dominance with a sophisticated, modern aesthetic.

02 — Fariolismo

Control & Chaos

The tactical identity is built on nerve. Farioli’s Porto seeks to hold the ball in deep zones, baiting the opponent to press before exploiting the space behind. It is a high-wire act that has yielded the best defensive record in the country. With 65% average possession, they do not just win; they suffocate.

03 — La Famiglia Portista

Gravitational Center

When the net ripples, the tactical geometry dissolves. The goal celebrations in this era are distinct—magnetic collisions of bodies that gravitate toward the touchline. Farioli is rarely a distant observer; he is often engulfed in the collective. This physical connection demonstrates that the 16 wins are not achieved by instruction alone, but by a unified pulse.

04 — The Spearhead

Ruthless Efficiency

At the tip of the spear is Samu Aghehowa. His 19 goals have provided the ruthless finishing touch to the team's intricate build-up. While the system is complex, the outcome is simple. Behind him, Diogo Costa has evolved into a true "Sweeper-Keeper," operating as an eleventh outfield player to sustain the high line.

05 — The Horizon

Sixteen of Seventeen

The quarter-final victory over Benfica was a masterclass in adaptation, silencing the skeptics. Sitting atop the league with 16 wins in 17 matches, the trajectory is clear. The Dragon has been reborn, not through fire and fury alone, but through intellect, design, and unity.
